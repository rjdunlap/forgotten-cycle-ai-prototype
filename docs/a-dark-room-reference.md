# A Dark Room Reference

Last ingested: 2026-05-03

This document captures Robert's supplied research synthesis on `A Dark Room` as a design reference for the Forgotten Cycle AI prototype. Treat it as a feel and structure bible for minimalist unfolding play, especially the first coastal sequence. It is not a command to reproduce `A Dark Room` beat for beat.

## Status And Scope

- Source status: user-provided deep research synthesis.
- Verification status: not independently fact-checked against original source code, developer commentary, or platform-specific versions.
- Current prototype scope: use this mainly to tune "The First Ember" and the early shore, not to rush toward town building, combat, slavery, starships, or cosmic reveals.
- IP caution: this is inspiration and analysis. Do not copy exact copy, event names, narrative twists, or progression tables into a public game.

## Core Lesson

`A Dark Room` works because it begins with almost nothing, gives the player one meaningful action, and lets the interface unfold only after the player has sat with the situation.

The important early feeling is:

- cold and disorientation before competence
- a single grounded action before a menu
- short action timers that create rhythm without explaining the whole machine
- clean empty space that asks the player to imagine what is missing
- clipped, sensory copy that suggests more world than it states
- mechanical expansion that feels like discovery, not onboarding

For this project, the coast should borrow that rhythm. The player should not land inside a dashboard. They should first notice the shore, then understand the log, then recognize wood, fire, light, thirst, food, and danger as the mind becomes more organized.

## Opening Structure To Emulate

`A Dark Room` starts as a void with one action: light the fire. The fire is both mechanical and narrative. It saves, warms, marks time, and gives the player a reason to keep looking at the room.

Forgotten Cycle's coastal equivalent should be:

- initial state: washed ashore, no coherent log yet
- first action: `Get Your Bearings`
- first reveal: the mind makes a clean mark, forming `Entry I`
- second reveal: light, body state, wreckage, and wood signs
- first survival loop: gather wood, tend fire, endure night
- first death: not only failure, but the first useful payout
- first wake: repeated sensory anchor plus a small impossible prediction

The opening should remain quiet. Let the player suspect there is a system before showing the system.

## Pacing Implications

The research calls out the importance of the early delay before `A Dark Room` expands. The exact timing does not matter here as much as the principle:

> The player needs enough stillness to feel stranded, but not so much delay that the prototype feels broken.

Current todo direction aligns well with this:

- a compact opening shell before the log exists
- `Get Your Bearings` as the only initial action
- gradual readout reveals at early Bearings levels
- no visible meta-currency or full prestige shop yet
- a speed toggle for testing so normal play can breathe

Use playtesting to tune the first 30-90 seconds around curiosity, not efficiency.

## Action Timer Feel

Action timers should feel like labor under uncertainty. They should not initially read as abstract XP machines.

Good early timer language:

- "Notice tide, wind, and the shape of the shore."
- "Search the broken line for dry wood the fire might accept."
- "Feed one piece of wood and learn whether the flame takes it."

Delay explicit math until the player has earned a system readout. Before then, action levels can be visible, but details should stay narrative.

## Interface As Story

The interface should be part of the fiction.

`A Dark Room` uses its sparse interface as a narrative device: the world is blank until the player has reason to perceive more. Forgotten Cycle should use the Ancestral Logbook the same way.

Concrete rules:

- Hide the log until the character can form an entry.
- Hide body/light readouts until the player has oriented.
- Hide fire until wood or fire is meaningfully present.
- Reveal places as inert hooks before turning them into full systems.
- Prefer "Entry I" and logbook framing over app-like cycle counters.
- Keep the first screen practical and text-forward.

## Minimalist Copy Pattern

The writing should be clipped and sensory. It should imply rather than explain.

Useful coastal motifs:

- three gull-cries and one broken wave
- salt wind
- tar-dark planks
- rope fiber
- crate corners
- bent nails
- dry needles under stone lips
- the jungle line going quiet
- firelight as a boundary

Avoid copy that makes the player competent too early. Early lines should feel observed, not mastered.

## Expansion Pattern

`A Dark Room` expands in genre layers:

1. single-room survival
2. resource loop
3. village labor system
4. map exploration
5. moral inversion and cosmic context

Forgotten Cycle's early version should only use the first two layers:

1. stranded-body survival
2. tiny wood/fire/logbook loop

Later expansion can borrow the same unfolding philosophy, but should not copy the village or slavery arc directly. If the project eventually adds settlement, faction, or expedition layers, each layer should arrive because the shore loop has made a new need legible.

## Moral And Thematic Warning

The supplied analysis emphasizes `A Dark Room`'s moral inversion: what begins as survival becomes exploitation, conquest, and eventually recognition that the player may have been the danger all along.

Forgotten Cycle does not need that exact twist, but it can keep the principle:

> Progress should leave traces. The player's increasing power should eventually make them ask what survival has cost.

For the first slice, the cost can remain smaller and more intimate:

- the body pays for each discovery
- the fire keeps one danger away while revealing another
- knowledge arrives through repeated death
- the shore becomes familiar because the player has failed there before

## Current Project Alignment

Strong matches already present:

- `Get Your Bearings` as the initial unfolding action
- delayed log, body, light, fuel, and place readouts
- repeated wake sensory anchor
- wood/fire separation instead of instant warmth fuel
- action rows with progress
- narrative detail before system math
- death as a payout and memory moment

Risks to watch:

- too many body meters too early can weaken the stark opening
- visible action levels can make the game feel like a training dashboard if copy turns numeric too soon
- place hooks can imply a bigger exploration game before the survival loop is satisfying
- speed/testing controls should stay clearly outside the fiction
- the first fire should feel like a discovery and a boundary, not just another resource converter

## Practical Guidance For The Coast

For the next few passes, use `A Dark Room` as a test:

- Does the first screen have one unmistakable action?
- Does each new panel feel earned by perception?
- Does waiting create tension instead of boredom?
- Does the copy leave enough empty space for imagination?
- Does death make the next entry feel uncanny and practical?
- Does fire become the emotional center of the first night?

If an addition does not strengthen one of those answers, park it.
